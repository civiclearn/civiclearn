The Justice System

The Canadian justice system guarantees everyone due process under the law. Our judicial system is founded on the presumption of innocence in criminal matters, which means everyone is innocent until proven guilty.

Canada’s legal system is based on a heritage that includes the rule of law, freedom under the law, democratic principles and due process. Due process is the principle that the government must respect all of the legal rights a person is entitled to under the law.

Canada has an organized system of laws. These laws are written rules meant to guide people in our society. They are made by elected representatives. The courts settle disputes and the police enforce the laws. In Canada the law applies to everyone, including judges, politicians and the police. Laws are meant to keep order in society, provide a peaceful way to settle disputes and express the values and beliefs of Canadians.

The courts

The Supreme Court of Canada is the country’s highest court. The Federal Court of Canada deals with matters involving the federal government. In most provinces there is an appeal court and a trial court (sometimes called the Court of King’s Bench — Court of Queen’s Bench in the guide — or the Supreme Court).

There are also provincial courts for lesser offences, as well as family courts, traffic courts and small claims courts for civil cases involving small sums of money.

The police

The police are there to keep people safe and to enforce the law. You can ask the police for help in many situations: if there has been an accident, if someone has stolen something from you, if you are a victim of assault, if someone is missing, or if you see a crime being committed.

There are different types of police in Canada:

  • Provincial police in Ontario and Quebec.
  • Municipal police departments in all provinces.
  • The Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP), which enforces federal laws throughout Canada and serves as the provincial police in all provinces and territories except Ontario and Quebec, as well as in some municipalities.

The police are there to help you. If needed, you can question the police about their service or conduct. Almost all police forces in Canada have a formal process for raising concerns or making a complaint.

Getting legal help

Lawyers can help you with legal problems and represent you in court. If you cannot pay for a lawyer, most communities offer legal aid services free of charge or at a low cost.
