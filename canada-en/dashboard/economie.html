Canada's Economy

A trading nation

Canada has always been a trading nation, and commerce remains the engine of its economic growth. Canadians could not keep up their standard of living without trading with the rest of the world.

In 1988, Canada signed a free trade agreement with the United States. Mexico became a partner in 1994 under the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA), which covered more than 444 million people and, in 2008, accounted for over $1 trillion in merchandise trade. On July 1, 2020, NAFTA was replaced by the Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement (CUSMA).

Today, Canada is one of the world’s ten largest economies and a member of the G7, together with the United States, Germany, the United Kingdom, Italy, France and Japan.

Canada’s three main types of industries

1. Service industries

Service industries are the largest sector in Canada. They provide thousands of jobs in areas such as transportation, education, health care, construction, banking, communications, retail, tourism and government. More than 75 % of working Canadians now have jobs in service industries.

2. Manufacturing industries

Manufacturing industries make goods to sell in Canada and around the world. Products include paper, high-technology equipment, aerospace technology, automobiles, machinery, food and clothing. The United States is our largest international trading partner.

3. Natural resource industries

This sector includes forestry, fishing, agriculture, mining and energy. These industries have played a central part in Canada’s history and development. Even today, they are the economic base of many regions and make up a large share of our exports.

Economic ties with the United States

Canada has very close economic ties with the United States, and each country is the other’s largest trading partner. Over three-quarters of Canada’s exports go to the U.S. Together, the two countries have the biggest bilateral trading relationship in the world.

Integrated Canada-U.S. supply chains compete with the rest of the world. Every year Canada exports billions of dollars’ worth of energy products, industrial goods, machinery and equipment, automotive products, agricultural products, fish, forestry products and consumer goods.

Every year, millions of Canadians and Americans cross safely what is often called “the world’s longest undefended border”.

A symbol of Canada-U.S. friendship

At Blaine, in the State of Washington, stands the Peace Arch, which bears the inscriptions:

“Children of a common mother”
“Brethren dwelling together in unity”

The monument symbolizes the close ties and common interests of the two nations.
